# Remote Blood-Pressure Monitoring and 90-Day Readmission in Adults with Hypertension: A Retrospective Cohort Study

## Abstract
We evaluated whether a remote blood-pressure monitoring program reduced 90-day hospital readmission among adults with hypertension. In a retrospective cohort of 142 patients, remote monitoring was associated with lower readmission risk (adjusted OR 0.62, 95% CI 0.41–0.94, p=0.024).

## Introduction
Hypertension remains a leading driver of cardiovascular hospitalization. Remote patient monitoring (RPM) programs are increasingly used, but evidence on short-term readmission remains mixed.

## Methods
We included adults (≥18 years) with a primary diagnosis of hypertension discharged from two community hospitals between January 2022 and June 2023. The exposure was enrollment in a Bluetooth-enabled home blood-pressure cuff program within 7 days of discharge. The primary outcome was all-cause hospital readmission within 90 days. We fit multivariable logistic regression adjusting for age, sex, Charlson comorbidity index, and baseline systolic blood pressure.

This was a retrospective analysis of electronic health record data. The protocol was reviewed by the institutional review board; waiver of informed consent was requested because the study used existing clinical data. A clinical trial registry number is not provided in this draft. Data use agreements with the health system are mentioned only generically.

## Results
The analytic sample included 142 patients (RPM: n=71; usual care: n=71). Mean age was 64.2 years (SD 11.8); 54% were women. The 90-day readmission rate was 18.3% in the RPM group versus 29.6% in usual care. After adjustment, RPM enrollment was associated with reduced readmission (OR 0.62, 95% CI 0.41–0.94, p=0.024).

## Discussion
Remote blood-pressure monitoring was associated with lower 90-day readmission and improved systolic control. Importantly, remote blood-pressure monitoring was linked to fewer return hospitalizations within three months, which may reflect tighter home titration and earlier clinical outreach. Residual confounding is possible because program enrollment was not randomized.

## Limitations
Single health-system data, short follow-up, and incomplete capture of out-of-network readmissions may bias estimates toward the null.
